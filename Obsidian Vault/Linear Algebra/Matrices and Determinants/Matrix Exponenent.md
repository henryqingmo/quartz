### Idea
![[Pasted image 20250816123712.png]]
Using [[Taylor series]] expansion for $e^{At}$, we can differentiate it to obtain $Ae^{At}$, which is exactly $A(e^{At})$.

![[Pasted image 20250816124725.png]]
Looking more closely at the expansion of $e^{At}$, we basically pullout V on the left and $V^{-1}$ on the right to obtain the result in an [[Eigenvalues and eigenvectors]] decomposition. Therefore $y$ can be rewritten as the expansion.
