> [!note]
> Since we are looking for the minimum number of fail for a success, the latter the success, the lower the chances, since that requires all of the outcomes before it to be fail. 

>[!question]
>That leads us into the **Gambler's fallacy**, when we have many fails, would that means the chance of success is higher ?, because from the graph the chance of not wining is very low. 
![[Pasted image 20250708163919.png]]
The answer is no, no matter how unlikely our past of loosing is, that's not gonna affect the future. The memoryless property assure that we starts fresh after the condition. 

> [!tip]
Think of it like this, the unlikeliness came from I have very large chance of at least winning once before this trial, but having then all loosing have nothing to do with my next trial. So this global view only works for the likeliness of the past but cannot guarantee the future.
