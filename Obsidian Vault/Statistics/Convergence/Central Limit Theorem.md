### Idea
![[Pasted image 20250715145943.png]]
> [!note]
>When we take sums of **iid** [[Statistics/Random Variables/Random Variable#Sum of a random variables|Random Variable]], by the [[Expected Value#Linearity of expectation|Linearity of Expectation]],  we will get a random variables with mean $n\mu$ and [[Variance and Standard Deviation|Variance]] $n\sigma^2$ (0 [[Covariance]]), That means if we take n to $\infty$ variance tends to infinity.
>![[Pasted image 20250715155313.png]]
>
>But If we instead take the [[Random Sampling#Sample Mean|sample mean]], with n to $\infty$, the result will be a degenerate distribution, where all points lies on the mean [[Random Sampling#Property|Property]].
>
> But if we divide the sum by $\sqrt{n}$ the limiting shape will take a shape of a [[Normal Distribution]], with the same mean and variance.

![[Pasted image 20250715153130.png]]
We can then [[Normal Distribution#Standardise|Standardise]] this normal distribution to a standard normal distribution.
