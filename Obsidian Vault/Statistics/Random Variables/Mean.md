### Idea
![[Pasted image 20250615141327.png]]
Mean or the average can be thought of as the amount that makes the difference cancel out.
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum\limits_{x=1}^{n} (x - \mu) &= 0 \\
\sum\limits_{x=1}^{n} x &= n\mu \\
\mu &= \frac{1}{n} \sum\limits_{x=1}^{n} x
\end{aligned}
$$
![[Assets/Pictures/MATH2901 Course Notes 1.png]]
[[MATH2901 Course Notes.pdf#page=35&rect=51,47,525,261|MATH2901 Course Notes, p.35]]
If there is a frequency $f$ associated with each $x$, such that $n = \sum\limits f$, we have
$$
\sum\limits_{x=1}^{n} \frac{f}{n}x =\sum\limits p_{X}(x)x
$$
