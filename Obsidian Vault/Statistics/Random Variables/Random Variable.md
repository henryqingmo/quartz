### Idea
![[Pasted image 20250614171520.png]]
A Random Variable $X: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$
Basically maps each outcome in $\Omega$ to real value $x = X(\omega)$
#### Example
![[Pasted image 20250614171402.png]]
Random Variable $W$ is a function that associates a student a value of weight. Similarly $H$ associates them with their height.
$B$ associates the outcome $w$ and $h$ to their body index.
#### Sum of a random variables 
![[Pasted image 20250715134333.png]]
> [!abstract]
In this example, each outcome $\vec \omega$  will be mapped to $\omega_{1}$ by $X_1$ and $\omega_2$ by $X_2$. And since this relationship holds for all $\omega \in \Omega$, we can treat the functions as variables.

![[Pasted image 20250715134728.png|500]]
So the [[Expected Value]] can be thought of as summing up the values of Z and the probability of the outcome over the whole sample space.
